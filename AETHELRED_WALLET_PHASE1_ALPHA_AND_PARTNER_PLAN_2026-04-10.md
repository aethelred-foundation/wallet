## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Phase 1 moves from internal alpha to controlled design-partner readiness.

It exists to prevent two common mistakes:

- calling something “alpha” without telemetry, support, or gates
- inviting partners before the wallet has a stable trust posture

## 2. Internal Alpha Objective

The internal alpha should prove:

- targeted first-party flows work reliably in Aethelred Wallet
- connect, session, sign, and approval experiences are understandable
- support and engineering can observe problems quickly
- the product is improving through evidence, not internal opinion

## 3. Alpha Entry Criteria

Internal alpha begins only when:

- the extension is stable for the target flows
- telemetry and diagnostics exist
- release checklist exists
- known critical trust issues are documented
- internal alpha cohort and support owners are named

## 4. Alpha Cohort Structure

Recommended cohort:

- internal product and engineering leads
- internal operators using first-party flows
- one or two power users who will stress repeated use
- support or ops representatives who can test response workflows

Recommended constraints:

- limit alpha to defined flows
- avoid broad open-ended testing in the first wave
- keep the cohort small enough for close support

## 5. Alpha Feedback Model

Every alpha issue should capture:

- surface
- user flow
- severity
- reproducibility
- trust or safety impact
- owner
- target fix window

Alpha review should happen weekly and answer:

- what broke
- what confused users
- what repeated most often
- what blocks broader use

## 6. Alpha Exit Criteria

The internal alpha is considered healthy when:

- first-party scoped flows work consistently
- high-severity defects are burned down
- users can complete key journeys without live guidance
- support can triage issues quickly
- no major trust issue remains unacknowledged

## 7. Design-Partner Objective

The design-partner phase should validate:

- whether Aethelred Wallet is truly preferable to incumbent wallets for targeted use cases
- whether the product can support external workflows without internal heroics
- whether the Phase 2 enterprise roadmap is grounded in real buyer behavior

## 8. Design-Partner Candidate Profile

Best early partner profiles:

- teams already close to Aethelred workflows
- users with high tolerance for controlled product change
- teams with real need for connect, approval, or safety improvements

Avoid first:

- highly bespoke users needing enterprise controls not yet delivered
- open-ended pilots with no measurable workflow goal
- partners expecting sovereign-grade deployment in Phase 1

## 9. Partner Entry Criteria

Partners should not be invited until:

- internal alpha signals are stable enough
- onboarding material exists
- scope and unsupported areas are explicit
- trust posture and known limitations are documented
- success metrics are agreed in advance

## 10. Partner Onboarding Pack

The partner pack should include:

- supported flows
- known limitations
- onboarding steps
- support channel
- escalation path
- review cadence
- feedback expectations

## 11. Partner Success Metrics

Measure at minimum:

- first successful connect rate
- first successful signed action rate
- session reliability
- approval comprehension
- incumbent-wallet displacement for targeted flows
- support burden per partner

## 12. Phase 1 Exit Review Inputs

The Phase 1 exit review should include:

- product demo
- alpha metrics
- top fixed issues
- top unresolved issues
- support summary
- design-partner readiness summary
- Phase 2 recommendation and dependency list
