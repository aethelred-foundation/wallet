# Contributor Code of Conduct

> **Last updated:** 2026-04-19
> **Owner:** Ramesh Tamilselvan — `conduct@aethelred.org`
> **Applies to:** `aethelred-foundation/wallet` and any community space
> operated in its name (GitHub discussions, issues, PRs, security
> reports under `.github/SECURITY.md`, and any future Discord / Slack
> community channels).

This Code of Conduct adapts the **Contributor Covenant v2.1** with
additions that reflect the Aethelred Wallet's position as a
compliance-native, security-sensitive project. It partially closes
`docs/compliance/SOC2_SCOPE.md` §5 item #5 (formal code of conduct for
CC1.1).

## 1. Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation
in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless
of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## 2. Our standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Accepting responsibility and apologising to those affected by our
  mistakes, and learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the
  overall community.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## 3. Aethelred-specific additions

These extend the Covenant to reflect the security and compliance
posture of this project.

### 3.1 Responsible disclosure takes precedence over public criticism

If you find a security vulnerability, follow
`.github/SECURITY.md` — do not open a public issue, comment on a PR, or
post on social media until disclosure is coordinated. This applies
whether you find the issue through your own research, while reviewing
a PR, or during regular use of the wallet.

Public "dunking" on security bugs — including those by or about
contributors — is not acceptable conduct. Constructive critique is
always acceptable; it simply belongs in the responsible-disclosure
channel until the fix ships.

### 3.2 Attribution policy — humans only

All contributions to this repository must be attributable to a named
human committer and reviewer. This means:

- Commits must be authored and signed by a natural person, with a
  verifiable GitHub account and a valid commit signature.
- PRs must be opened by a natural person who takes personal
  responsibility for the content.
- **Co-author, author, reviewer, or "generated by" attributions to AI
  agents, large-language-model providers, automation bots, or any
  non-human identity are not accepted** in commit messages, PR
  descriptions, review comments, or documentation.
- Tooling that assists a human (IDEs, linters, formatters, copilots,
  code generators, refactoring tools) is fine to use; the human
  operator is the author of record and is responsible for every line
  that lands.
- Automated vendor PRs (Dependabot, Renovate) are the single exception
  and are identified by their dedicated bot accounts only.

Rationale: contributions touching signing paths, audit chains, and
policy evaluation must have a clear human owner for both audit
evidence (SOC-2 CC1.5, CC8.1) and regulatory accountability. "The bot
did it" is not a defensible answer to an auditor or a regulator.

Violations of this policy are grounds for having a PR closed and, for
repeated violations, loss of committer privileges.

### 3.3 No hidden self-dealing

Contributors must disclose any material financial interest in a dApp,
token, protocol, or vendor relevant to a change they are proposing.
Disclosure goes in the PR description. Failing to disclose is a
serious integrity issue and subject to the enforcement ladder in §5.

### 3.4 Evidence over opinion in technical discussion

For changes touching `packages/core`, `packages/audit`,
`packages/policy`, `packages/approval`, or `packages/compliance`,
reviewers are expected to ground comments in specifications,
standards, or reproducible test cases. "This feels wrong" is fine as
a signal to investigate, but a blocking review requires pointing to
the exact contract being violated.

## 4. Enforcement responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing the
standards of acceptable behaviour and will take appropriate and fair
corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit,
or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions
that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and will communicate
reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## 5. Enforcement ladder

| Step | Action | Trigger |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1. Correction | Private, written clarification from a community leader. No public note. | Unintentional or minor issue, first instance. |
| 2. Warning | Private written warning with consequences stated. Continued behaviour leads to step 3. | Deliberate violation, or repeat of step 1. |
| 3. Temporary ban | Timed ban (typically 14–90 days) from all community spaces. | Serious or sustained violation. |
| 4. Permanent ban | Permanent removal from all community spaces. | Pattern of step 3 violations, harassment, or a single egregious incident (e.g. doxxing, threat). |

Security-specific violations (§3.1) can jump to step 3 or 4 for a
first incident if the disclosure breach materially harmed users.

Attribution-policy violations (§3.2) start at step 1 (close the PR,
explain the rule) and escalate to step 2 on repeat.

## 6. Reporting

Report issues to **`conduct@aethelred.org`**. Reports will be reviewed
and investigated promptly and fairly. The privacy and safety of the
reporter will be respected; reporters are never named in corrective
communication without consent.

For security vulnerabilities, use `security@aethelred.org` per
`.github/SECURITY.md`, not the conduct channel.

For legal matters, contact `legal@aethelred.org`.

## 7. Scope

This Code applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an
individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.
Examples of representing our community include using an official email
address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an
appointed representative at an online or offline event.
