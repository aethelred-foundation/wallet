## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the wallet program moves from sovereign preparation into the first sovereign or equivalent high-assurance pilot.

It exists to prevent two common Phase 3 mistakes:

- calling a pilot “sovereign” without explicit deployment, control, and evidence assumptions
- launching the pilot without rehearsing incident, support, and review paths

## 2. Pilot Objective

The sovereign or equivalent high-assurance pilot should validate:

- whether dedicated deployment and isolation assumptions hold in practice
- whether sovereign policy controls and higher-assurance approvals are usable
- whether restricted app distribution works in the intended environment
- whether review artifacts are strong enough for high-assurance stakeholders

## 3. Pilot Entry Criteria

The pilot should begin only when:

- deployment profile and isolation assumptions are explicit
- residency and custody controls are documented
- high-assurance approval modes are usable
- sovereign-only app distribution path exists
- evidence-grade review artifacts are ready
- incident and support owners are named

## 4. Recommended Pilot Shape

The first pilot should be:

- tightly scoped
- highly controlled
- limited to named users and workflows
- rich in observation and evidence capture

It should not be:

- an open-ended launch
- a broad public pilot
- a substitute for unfinished sovereign design work

## 5. Pilot Participants

Recommended pilot participants:

- named operator users
- named approvers or reviewers
- named admin or deployment owners
- named support and incident owners
- named security reviewer

## 6. Pilot Runbook Expectations

The pilot runbook should include:

- supported workflows
- stop conditions
- escalation path
- evidence capture expectations
- support ownership
- review cadence

## 7. Pilot Success Metrics

Measure at minimum:

- controlled workflow completion rate
- approval completion rate under high-assurance mode
- evidence completeness
- support burden during pilot
- severity and frequency of pilot issues
- operator comprehension of controls

## 8. Rehearsal Requirements

Before launch, the team should rehearse:

- end-to-end operator flow
- end-to-end approval flow
- evidence review flow
- support escalation flow
- pilot stop or rollback decision path

## 9. Launch Window Rules

The pilot launch window should:

- have a named go or no-go decision owner
- have a named issue triage owner
- have live telemetry and evidence monitoring
- avoid casual scope expansion during the launch window

## 10. Pilot Exit Review Inputs

The pilot exit review should include:

- workflow results
- control and policy behavior
- evidence and reporting quality
- incident summary
- operator and reviewer feedback
- capability gaps for the next roadmap horizon

## 11. Phase 3 Exit Criteria

Phase 3 should exit only when:

- a controlled sovereign or equivalent pilot has actually run
- the team can explain what worked and what did not
- the next roadmap priorities are based on pilot evidence
- the program closes with continuity rather than a planning cliff
